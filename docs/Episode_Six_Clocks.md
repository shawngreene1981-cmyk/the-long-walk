# THE RESET — Episode: "Six Clocks" (working title) — How six civilizations counted back to the beginning

**Status:** OUTLINE, 2026-11-25 (full version in the claude.ai project at episodes/Episode_Six_Clocks.md). Dates per RESEARCH_Synchronoptic_Lanes_Sourcing.md.
**On-screen device:** the Synchronoptic Lanes panel under the Long Walk map. This episode is the panel's debut, so these are the beats the panel must hit on camera.

## THE QUESTION
Six civilizations each looked back and asked when it all began, and each built a clock to answer. Lay the six side by side with the ground: how much happened before the stories begin, and why do so many stories begin in roughly the same place?

## OPENING VISUAL (panel beat 1)
The lanes slide up under the map for the first time. The scrubber runs from deep time forward; the region lanes fill in (footprints, corridors, Göbekli Tepe alone at ~9600 BCE). The six tradition lanes stay dark and hatched. As the scrubber passes 5509/4004 (Biblical), 3114 (Maya), 3102 (Hindu), Dynasty 1 / Manetho, the Sumerian list, and the Yellow Emperor, the six clocks switch on one by one. Freeze with all six lit and Göbekli five thousand years to their left, in the dark.

## COLD OPEN
1650: Ussher counts patriarchs to October 4004 BC. Not a crank: the best arithmetic his sources allowed, and he wasn't the only one. Quick cuts: an Indian astronomer fixing the age of strife; a Maya scribe carving a date counted from thousands of years before him; a Babylonian scribe copying kings who ruled for millennia before the Flood. "Six civilizations built a clock to find the beginning. Tonight we put all six on one wall."

## ACT 1 — WHAT A CLOCK IS
A reckoning is an artifact with a maker, purpose and date. Adams's 1871 chart: 23 feet from 4004 BC. Then its left edge, where it stops. (Panel beat 2: the hatched "before this reckoning" zone.)

## ACT 2 — THE SIX CLOCKS (one segment each, same template)
Who set it, when, from what, where it starts, what happened first, where it hands over to dated record. Each ends with its lane lighting.
1. Biblical (Ussher/Adams; other reckonings 3761–5509 BC).
2. Hindu (yugas; Kali epoch 3102 BCE, a theoretical conjunction; the deep cycle off the edge of the map).
3. Maya (Long Count zero 3114 BCE; first carved 36/32 BCE).
4. Chinese (Yellow Emperor, Xia; oracle bones 1254–1197; year-precise 841 BCE).
5. Egyptian (Manetho's gods and demigods; Abydos list; Dynasty 1, Aha 3111–3045 by radiocarbon).
6. Sumerian (8 kings, 241,200 years; the Flood; Kish).

## ACT 3 — THE WALL (panel beat 3: simultaneity)
Scrub the full panel: what each clock said when the pyramids rose, when the Indus cities peaked. The cluster near 3100 BCE (Maya and Hindu 12 years apart, Egypt's Dynasty 1 beside them: one pixel on the map, separated in the tooltip). The two Floods side by side.

## ACT 4 — THE DIVERGENCE TEST
The ~3100 cluster: independent clocks, fixed centuries later by different methods; ~3100 BCE is when writing and states appear. Ussher's Flood (2348 BC) vs Egypt and Sumer's continuous records. The Floods: a known literary inheritance. What no clock can see: Göbekli, the Tas Tepeler, the Long Walk. Verdicts by tier on screen.

## ACT 5 — WHERE MEMORY STARTS
The clocks don't record the beginning of the world; they record the beginning of remembering it. The hatched zone on every lane, everything before memory, is this show's territory. Close on the opening frame. "Every civilization counted back to the beginning. None of them could count that far."
