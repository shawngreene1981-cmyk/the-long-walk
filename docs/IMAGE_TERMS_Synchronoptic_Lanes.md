## CLEARED — public domain or CC0, no condition this project cannot meet

### Egyptian — the Abydos King List

- **File:** `File:Abydos King List.jpg` (Wikimedia Commons)
- **What:** the king list in the temple of Seti I at Abydos, 76 kings from Menes.
- **Author:** Markh, photographed 2004. 960 × 1,280, 849 KB, JPEG.
- **Terms, as the page states them:** *"This work has been released into the public domain
  by its author, Markh at English Wikipedia. This applies worldwide."* With a fallback
  grant of unconditional use where a release is not legally possible.
- **Condition on us:** none. Credited anyway.
- §4a of the design offered "the Turin **or** Abydos list"; Abydos is the one the lane
  already cites, so the image and the lane agree.

### Sumerian — the Weld-Blundell Prism

- **File:** `File:Sumeriankinglist.jpg` (Wikimedia Commons)
- **What:** the Sumerian King List itself — Ashmolean AN1923.444, Larsa, c. 1827–1817 BC.
  The object the `skl-prism` item on the lane already points at.
- **Author:** 俞磊, 2006. **320 × 589, 81 KB** — small.
- **Terms:** released into the public domain by its author, worldwide, with the same
  unconditional-use fallback.
- **Condition on us:** none.
- **Caveat:** at 320 px wide this is low resolution. Behind a lane at 10–15 % opacity that
  is survivable; it would not stand being shown large. The better-resolution alternatives
  on Commons are CC BY-SA 4.0 (see below), which is why this one is preferred.

### Chinese — an oracle bone from Anyang

- **File:** `File:Ancient Chinese Writing on Ox Scapula, Shang Dynasty Oracle Bone, Yinxu,
  Anyang.jpg` (Wikimedia Commons)
- **What:** ox scapula with Shang divination writing, Chinese Writing Museum, Anyang.
  The lane's `cn-oracle` item is the Wu Ding corpus from this site.
- **Author:** Gary Todd, 2011, via Flickr. 3,456 × 5,184, 3.2 MB.
- **Terms:** **CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication**, confirmed by Commons'
  FlickreviewR 2 bot on 14 December 2020.
- **Condition on us:** none. CC0 asks for nothing, including attribution. Credited anyway.

### Maya — Quiriguá Stela C, east side

- **File:** `File:East side of stela C, Quirigua.PNG` (Wikimedia Commons)
- **What:** **the Long Count's own zero carved in stone** — 13.0.0.0.0, 4 Ahau 8 Cumku,
  11 August 3114 BCE. This is the lane's `lc-zero` item as a Maya monument records it,
  which is a better fit than a photograph of any site.
- **Author:** Maudslay's drawing, published in Cyrus Thomas, *Mayan Calendar Systems II*
  (1904). 251 × 579, 29 KB, PNG.
- **Terms:** public domain — author's life plus 70 years or fewer (Maudslay died 1931,
  so the drawing cleared in 2001), and published before 1 January 1931, so public domain
  in the United States.
- **Condition on us:** none.
- **Caveat Commons raises:** a few countries run longer terms, Guatemala 75 years among
  them, and Quiriguá is in Guatemala. The 1904 publication clears 75 years as well.

### Biblical — Adams' Illustrated Panorama of History

- **File:** `File:1878 Adams Monumental Illustrated Panorama of History - Geographicus -
  WorldHistory-adams-1871.jpg` (Wikimedia Commons)
- **What:** the chart this lane is drawn after. 27 inches by 260 inches, beginning at the
  creation of Adam in 4004 BC.
- **Author:** Sebastian C. Adams (1825–1898); scan provided to Commons by Geographicus
  Rare Antique Maps under a cooperation project. 10,000 × 4,235, **14.71 MB**.
- **Terms:** PD-Art — a faithful photographic reproduction of a two-dimensional public
  domain work; the work itself is public domain (life + 100 or fewer) and public domain in
  the United States as published before 1 January 1931.
- **Condition on us:** none.
- **TWO THINGS TO DECIDE BEFORE USING IT.**
  1. **This is the 1878 THIRD edition, not the 1871 first**, whatever the filename says —
     the page states it plainly. The lane says "as charted by Adams (1871)". Either the
     caption on the image says 1878, or a scan of the first edition is found instead.
  2. **14.71 MB** is more than this repository should carry for a background at 12 %
     opacity. It needs downscaling and re-encoding before it goes anywhere near the repo,
     and the credit must then say the file has been resized from the Commons original.

## NOT CLEARED — the Hindu lane

**No image for the yuga cycle has terms this project can accept, so under the ruling that
lane runs without one.**

- The only Sūrya-Siddhānta page image on Commons —
  `File:1st verse of the 1st chapter of the Surya Siddhanta Hindu astronomy, 1847 Sanskrit
  manuscript edition.jpg`, by Ms Sarah Welch — is **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Share-alike is the
  problem, not attribution: it asks that anything built on it be released under the same
  or a compatible licence, and this panel is not going to make a licence claim about
  itself in order to carry one background image.
- The yuga and kalpa diagrams in that category are **modern charts by Commons users**, not
  period images, so they would fail the brief even where the licence allowed them.
- The Kalachakra material is **Tibetan Buddhist, not Hindu**, and one of the two is sourced
  from Sotheby's.

**One candidate not yet run down**, recorded so the next person does not start over:
`File:English translation of the Surya Siddhanta and the Siddhanta Siromani by Sastri,
1861.djvu` on Commons — an 1861 printed edition, 309 pages, 23.24 MB, which would be public
domain on age. A single page would have to be extracted and re-encoded from the DjVu.
Whether an 1861 English translation counts as a *period* image for a reckoning computed
more than a thousand years earlier is a separate question, and the honest answer is
probably not.

## WHAT SHIPPED — dimensions, crops and changes

**Founder-ruled 2026-11-26: the five downloads approved; the Hindu lane runs without
an image; the Adams 1878 third-edition scan is to be used with the edition stated in
the credit, downscaled, and the source and output dimensions recorded here; the 320 px
prism file is to be used, with a non-blocking open item to replace it.**

Built by `geowork/lanes/build_lane_images.py`, which writes `img/lanes/manifest.json`
beside the files. The panel and `build_credits.py` both read that manifest, so a credit
cannot drift from the bytes it describes.

| file | source | crop (source px) | output | opacity | size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `adams-panorama.webp` | 3840×1626 | 0,190 – 3840,830 (3840×640) | 1200×200 | 0.155 | 68 KB |
| `abydos-kinglist.webp` | 960×1280 | 0,170 – 960,330 (960×160) | 1200×200 | 0.260 | 63 KB |
| `anyang-oracle-bone.webp` | 1670×1714 | 140,400 – 1090,558 (950×158) | 1200×200 | 0.170 | 24 KB |
| `weld-blundell-prism.webp` | 320×589 | 60,180 – 272,215 (212×35) | 1200×200 | 0.224 | 18 KB |
| `quirigua-stela-c.webp` | 251×579 | 0,300 – 251,342 (251×42) | 1200×200 | 0.100 | 17 KB |

**All five are cropped to 6:1 and resized to that same ratio, so none is distorted** —
the build aborts if a crop is not within 6 % of the output ratio. An earlier version
forced the width to 1200 while capping the height at 150, which silently squashed every
image by up to 3×; a squashed scan is not a faithful reproduction, and faithful
reproduction is the basis on which two of these are free to use at all.

**Why 6:1 and not the shape of the lane.** The band behind a tradition lane is about
650 × 30 px, roughly 21:1. No photograph of a stela or a prism is that shape, so
something must crop it; choosing the crop here rather than letting the browser take
whatever is in the middle of the frame is how each lane ends up carrying the part of
its object worth carrying.

**The opacities are measured, not chosen.** At one value for all five the Maya lane
dominated the panel: an inverted line drawing has more than twice the luminance spread
of a photograph of carved stone (sd 109 against a median of 52), so the same 17 % put
three times the ink on that row. Each opacity is now scaled by that strip’s own
contrast against the median and clamped to 0.10–0.26. "No lane privileged" is a ruling
about the data; it should hold for the decoration too.

**ONE SUBSTITUTION, AND IT IS NOT THE FILE THAT WAS APPROVED.** The approved Chinese
image was `File:Ancient Chinese Writing on Ox Scapula, Shang Dynasty Oracle Bone,
Yinxu, Anyang.jpg` (Gary Todd, CC0). It was downloaded, and the photograph turns out to
show the **uninscribed face** of the bone through museum glass — cracks and reflections,
no legible writing — so it would have put a blank bone behind the one lane whose point
is the earliest Chinese writing. It was replaced with **`File:Chinese oracle bone
(16th-10th C BC) - BL Or. 7694.jpg`**: British Library Or. 7694/1535v, held and digitised
by the British Library, uploaded to Flickr Commons, **CC0 1.0**, 1,670 × 1,714, 953 KB,
licence read on its own file page. It is a flat scan of the pitted and inscribed face,
with the poker burns that produced the cracks and several characters visible. Same
licence class, same subject, better evidence — but it is a change to the approved set
and is recorded here as one.

**The Adams edition, as ruled.** The scan is the **1878 third edition**; the lane caption
keeps "as charted by Adams (1871)", which is when the chart was first published, and the
image credit states the edition and that the file was downscaled and re-encoded from the
Commons scan. The Commons original is 10,000 × 4,235; this build fetched the 3,840 px
rendition from the Commons thumbnail service rather than pulling 14.7 MB to throw most
of it away, and cropped and re-encoded from that.

**The Hindu lane ships with no image and nothing on the panel pretends otherwise.**
