# RESEARCH — THE WORLD AT A MOMENT, THE SOURCING PASS

**2026-11-27.** Part B rules: real sources, a tier on every claim, **nothing typed
without a source**. The entries themselves are in `data/moments/moments.json`, written
by `geowork/moment/build_moments.py`; this records what the pass found and what it
refused to write.

## 1. THE SOURCED ENTRIES ADDED, AND WHERE THEY CAME FROM

### Southeast Asia at 30 CE — the region that was empty at every moment

- **Cổ Loa, the rampart ditch in use** — *contested*. Kim, Lai Văn Tới & Trịnh Hoàng
  Hiệp, *Antiquity* **84** (326), 2010, 1011–1027. Rampart Phase 3 layers calibrate to
  396–170 cal BC; **charcoal from the ditch to 103 BC – cal AD 67**, which brackets this
  moment. The tier is contested because the excavators say in their own words that the
  dates bound construction and use, **not the first digging**.
- **Lai Nghi, a Sa Huỳnh jar-burial cemetery** — *solid*. Reinecke and Lâm Thị Mỹ Dung;
  *IPPA Bulletin* **29**, 2009, 68–75, and Lâm Thị Mỹ Dung, *Terra Australis* **45**,
  2017. One jar dates to **2086 ± 50 BP (Erl-5995)** and the cemetery calibrates to the
  3rd century BC – 1st century AD.
- **Óc Eo begins** — *contested*. Wang et al., *Science Advances* **9**, 2023,
  eadh5517: the radiocarbon dates place the major occupation **between the 1st and 8th
  centuries CE**, and the single published sample calibrates to 207–326 cal CE. The port
  the Chinese sources call Funan. **Where inside the first century it starts is not
  fixed**, and that is the whole reason for the tier.

### Oceania — the finding, not the blank

- **The settled record pauses** — *solid*. Wilmshurst, Hunt, Lipo & Anderson, *PNAS*
  **108**, 2011, 1815–1820, from **1,434 radiocarbon dates across at least 45 islands**.
  West Polynesia is settled by c. 800 BCE and then migration into East Polynesia begins
  only **after a pause of about 1,800 years**, in two phases: the Society Islands c.
  1025–1120 CE and the rest c. 1190–1290 CE. Mulrooney et al., *PNAS* 108, E192, dispute
  the statistical model while accepting the late chronology, and that is recorded with
  it.

  **This refines the wording of the ruling.** "The settled record resumes c. 900 CE" is
  true of Micronesia — this map's own Nan Madol anchor is 900 CE — but on the
  high-precision dating **East Polynesia resumes c. 1025 CE**. Both are in the entry.

### East Asia at the Great Pyramid

- **Longshan, the late phase** — *solid*, c. 3000–1900 BC with a late phase c. 2600–2000
  BC, at the type site of Chengziya. From the Bayesian review of Chinese Neolithic
  radiocarbon dates held at Oxford (`ora.ox.ac.uk`), **which states its own caveat**: the
  dated samples do not necessarily catch the earliest and latest phases, so the
  boundaries are approximations.
- **Shijiahe, on the middle Yangtze** — *solid*, c. 2500–2000 BC, with Qujialing before
  it at c. 3400–2500 BC, from the same review. Contemporary with Liangzhu and culturally
  distinct from it.

  **Liangzhu itself does not fit this window and is not forced into it.** Its hydraulic
  system dates to c. 5,200–4,800 BP (Liu et al., *PNAS* 2017) — c. 3250–2850 BCE, three
  centuries before the Great Pyramid — and the map's existing Liangzhu anchor is 2309
  BCE, two centuries after it.

### The contemporaries of 30 CE

- **Rome under Tiberius** — *solid*, r. 14–37 CE (Britannica). The prefect who tried the
  case answers to this emperor, which is the only reason the year can be argued at all.
- **The Han restored, the capital at Luoyang** — *solid*, Guangwu r. 25–57 CE
  (Britannica), capital moved east in 25 CE.
- **Parthia under Artabanus II** — *contested*, r. c. 12–38/40 CE. The regnal dates are
  reconstructed from coins, Tacitus and Josephus, and scholars differ by several years
  at both ends.
- **Kujula Kadphises founds the Kushan empire** — *contested*, reign estimated c. 25–85
  CE. The Rabatak inscription fixes the dynasty's genealogy but **not its absolute
  dates**: the Kanishka era is placed at 127 CE on Harry Falk's work or at 78 CE on the
  older view, which is still argued (*Sino-Platonic Papers* 227, 2012). A fifty-year
  range, and the panel says so.
- **Cahuachi, the Nasca ceremonial centre** — *solid*. Silverman, *Cahuachi in the
  Ancient Nasca World*, covering c. 200 BC – AD 600, with Orefici's excavations showing
  it was **not a permanent domestic settlement**; Conlee et al. compile 770 radiocarbon
  dates from more than sixty south-coast sites against the ceramic phase chronology.
- **Kush and Rome, after the treaty of Samos** — *solid*. The treaty of 21/20 BCE (22 BC
  in some accounts) after Amanirenas' war with Petronius; Augustus withdrew the frontier
  to Hiere Sycaminos and Kush paid no tribute. **The bronze head of Augustus found
  buried beneath a temple step at Meroë** (Garstang, 1910) is the physical end of that
  episode.
- **Teotihuacan** and **Aksum** were already in the map's own anchors and are pointed at
  rather than retyped.

## 3. THE ENTRIES ARE NOT NEW MAP ANCHORS, AND THAT IS A RULING TO MAKE

A sourced entry in `moments.json` **is not added to the map's 565 dated anchors.** The
anchors are an approved set and quietly growing them by a dozen while building a panel
is what the approved-set rule of 2026-11-26 exists to stop.

So the eleven new sourced entries live in the moment data and appear in the panel, and
the map's own record is unchanged. **Whether they should also become anchors — pins on
the map at every date, not only at their moment — is worth ruling on.** My
recommendation: yes for the seven that are places with coordinates and a dated deposit
(Cổ Loa, Lai Nghi, Óc Eo, Longshan/Chengziya, Shijiahe, Cahuachi, Meroë), and no for the
four that are statements about a polity rather than a dated find (Tiberius, Guangwu,
Artabanus, Kujula) — those are context, and the map's anchors are evidence.
