The Long Walk, interactive: network, ghosts & reconvergence

A real, pannable, zoomable map — not the fixed schematic — scrubbing through deep time. This build demonstrates three new mechanics in one place: a pulsing Green Sahara corridor that grows and shrinks with the actual humid-period record, African origin nodes that appear on their own dated evidence instead of one fixed pin, and "ghost branches" — lineages that fade to grey and vanish to a faint marker when they die out, with a new bright marker lighting the same ground later when someone else arrives.
COMPLETE BUILD — the entire researched dataset is now on one scrubbable timeline: 552 dated anchors across five evidence tiers, 111 researched migration routes, three forced routes, and one text lineage — including the Siberian spine that carries the Ancient North Eurasian line to the Arctic — drawing progressively across their own real date windows, 15 gold leader walk-back lines, and 6 African corridor connectors linking the origin nodes so the story no longer starts abruptly with the southern migration. The timeline reaches back 1.8 million years, so the contested Gibraltar hominin claims and the pre-sapiens Luzon evidence sit at real dated positions rather than floating outside it. Roughly 20% of this map is flagged contested — and that is the point, not a defect. The aim is to show how well each claim is supported, not to assert what happened. A contested anchor is drawn as prominently as a solid one, in a different colour, so you can see the shape of the disagreement instead of having it quietly resolved for you.

What this prototype is actually showing

The Sahara corridor pulses instead of sitting on the map as a static line, because it wasn't a static line. A 2023 Nature Communications paleoclimate reconstruction counts 20 North African Humid Periods over the past 800,000 years, recurring roughly every 20,000–40,000 years. This prototype animates that on/off cycle directly, keyed to the specific dates already established in this project's own Correction 3 research: broadly viable 246,000–96,000 ya, with two final documented closing pulses around 78,000 and 67,000 ya before the corridor shuts for good. Scrub slowly through that range and watch the ring grow and shrink with each cycle, rather than reading it as one bridge crossed once.

The four origin nodes replace the single fixed Rift Valley pin from the old schematic build, each keyed to its own dated evidence: Jebel Irhoud, Morocco (~315,000 ya); the Rift/Horn, Ethiopia (~250,000 ya, this project's own long-running anchor); the Kalahari/Makgadikgadi, Botswana (~200,000 ya, the Chan et al. 2019 claim — shown CONTESTED, per Correction #6, not as a settled single origin); and the Ituri Forest, DRC (~170,000 ya). None of the four is "the" start. The Khoisan lineage's first split (~135,000 ya) appears as a persisting branch — it never ghosts, because it's a living population today — and a brief pulse around ~100,000 ya marks the merger that forms the ancestors of eastern and western African populations (Ragsdale et al. 2023).

Three ghost/reconvergence chains show the same visual grammar Shawn asked for, in two honest variants. Same exact ground: Jebel Faya's older occupation layer (~125,000–100,000 ya) fades to a ghost and leaves a faint marker — then the identical coordinates light back up around 65,000 ya when the Southern Route's successful pulse actually arrives. Dorset culture in the eastern Arctic does the same at the same coordinates around 1300 CE, replaced by Thule with "virtually no evidence of genetic or cultural interaction" between them — same spot, unrelated population. Same broader ground, not the same point: Tianyuan man's own lineage near Beijing (~40,000 ya) is a genuine dead end — nobody alive today descends from him — and fades to a ghost accordingly; the marker that lights up later, around 9,000 ya, sits nearby in the Yellow River valley and represents the Neolithic farmers whose ancestry actually became dominant across China. That's a different population arriving in the same region generations later, not the same line returning — worth keeping that distinction honest on-air rather than letting "reconvergence" imply a bloodline reunion it isn't.

Known limitation, stated plainly: the routes now carry the full researched multi-stage paths from human_spread_map_2.html, but the dedicated Kelp Highway, Southern Road and African Migrations maps still hold detail this one doesn't — landmark-level stops, then-and-now tables, and per-route discussion. This map answers "when, and in what order"; those answer "where, exactly, and what's there now." Route date windows are drawn from the project's own sourced research; where a window is genuinely disputed the underlying anchor carries the CONTESTED tag, but the line itself does not yet visually distinguish a contested window from a settled one. That's the next honest improvement to make here.
